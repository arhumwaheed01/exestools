# Friendsgiving and Thanksgiving team games (without the awkward picks)

By Arhum Waheed · Published 2026-11-05 · Updated 2026-11-05

Friendsgiving and Thanksgiving both have the same soft problem: a mixed room of people who know some faces and not others, a stretch of time before or after the meal, and someone who says "should we play a game?" Captain picks and "you two go over there" leave the same people standing alone. A fair random split fixes that in about thirty seconds, then the game itself can be as competitive or as silly as the room wants.

This guide is for hosts with roughly 8 to 24 guests. It covers how to split teams, which games fit a living room, and which ExesTools pages to open on a laptop before people arrive.

## Before anyone arrives: two lists on the laptop

Set these up the night before so you're not typing names while gravy reduces.

1. **A people list** in the [random team generator](/random-team-generator) or the [random name picker](/random-name-picker)—same names, one per line. First names are fine; add an initial if you have two Sams.
2. **Optional: a question or challenge list** on the [spinner wheel](/) for icebreakers, charades prompts or "who does the dishes" duties.

Both tools keep the list in the browser on that device. Fullscreen the page when you start so guests see the spin, not your tabs.

## How to split without leaving anyone out

Use the team generator and pick either **Number of teams** or **People per team**. Sizes stay within one person of each other. With 11 guests and 3 teams you get 4, 4, 3. With **Pairs**, an odd roster makes one trio so nobody sits alone.

Agree the rule out loud before the first deal:

- **Accept the first split**, or
- **One agreed reshuffle** if a couple lands together when you wanted them apart, or a quiet guest lands with three loud cousins.

Reshuffling until you get the teams you wanted is captain picking with extra steps. For skill-sensitive games (quiz night with two trivia sharks), run the [tiered method](/guides/how-to-make-balanced-random-teams) once: put the strongest players in a small tier the size of your team count so each team gets one, then deal everyone else.

The generator can't keep two people apart by rule. For a gift exchange where that matters, use the [Secret Santa generator](/secret-santa-generator) instead.

## Games that fit a holiday house

### 1. Cooking or prep stations (teams of 3–5)

**Time:** 20–40 minutes before dinner. **Ask:** low.

Split into stations: salad, sides, table setting, kids' activity table. Paste names, set the number of stations as teams, name the teams after the jobs, and generate. People who arrived early get a job without the host pointing. If one station finishes early, spin the [name picker](/random-name-picker) for who floats to help.

### 2. Friendsgiving trivia (2–4 teams)

**Time:** 25–40 minutes. **Ask:** medium.

Deal teams, then ask rounds: food science, holiday films, "who brought what," local geography. Keep scores on paper. Use the name picker with remove-winner on to choose which team answers first each round, so the same captain doesn't always shout first.

If the group is mostly strangers, write questions anyone can guess (pie flavors, Thanksgiving year myths) rather than inside jokes from one friend circle.

### 3. Charades or Pictionary (2 teams)

**Time:** 15–30 minutes. **Ask:** medium.

Two teams is enough. Load prompts on the homepage spinner ("mashed potatoes," "Black Friday," "the dog under the table") or keep a paper list and spin only for who acts next. Remove-winner on the people wheel means everyone performs once before repeats.

### 4. Thanksgiving bingo (any size)

**Time:** 10–20 minutes. **Ask:** very low.

Print cards from the [bingo card generator](/bingo-card-generator) with the **Thanksgiving** theme, or edit squares to match your house ("someone says 'I'm stuffed'," "a pie appears," "the dog gets turkey"). Spotting bingo during a holiday film needs no caller. For a hosted round, use the on-screen caller and agree a line win so games stay short between courses. List length and tie rules are in the [bingo ideas guide](/guides/bingo-games-for-classrooms-and-parties).

### 5. "Who does what" duties (whole group)

**Time:** 2 minutes. **Ask:** very low.

Put chores on a wheel: carve, pour drinks, clear plates, wrap leftovers, walk the dog. Spin the people wheel (remove-winner on) so duties rotate. Guests who hate a chore get one pass—spin again. It's lighter than asking for volunteers and fairer than always asking the same helpful person.

### 6. Leftover scavenger or photo bingo (kids and mixed ages)

**Time:** 10 minutes. **Ask:** low.

Edit a bingo card with rooms and objects ("candles," "orange napkin," "someone in a sweater"). Kids mark as they find them. Adults can play on phones with player links if you set those up ahead.

### 7. Gift exchange for friends (optional)

**Time:** 20–45 minutes. **Ask:** depends on the format.

- **Named gifts with couple exclusions:** [Secret Santa generator](/secret-santa-generator)—draw once, send private links or print slips.
- **Steal pile (Yankee swap / white elephant):** follow [white elephant gift exchange rules](/guides/white-elephant-gift-exchange-rules) and use the name picker with remove-winner on for turn order.

Pick one format. Running both the same night usually means one of them feels rushed.

### 8. Gratitude go-around (optional closer)

**Time:** 5–10 minutes. **Ask:** low to medium.

Spin for speaking order so nobody has to go first by volunteering. Keep the prompt soft: "one thing this year" or "one person here you're glad to see." Allow a pass. For more icebreaker shapes that don't make people cringe, see [icebreakers with a random picker](/guides/icebreaker-games-with-a-random-picker).

## A simple day plan

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Night before | Paste the guest list into the team generator; print bingo cards if you want them |
| Guests arrive | Name-picker duties for coats, drinks, music |
| 30–40 min before food | Cooking stations or a short bingo round |
| After the main course | Trivia or charades with the pre-dealt teams |
| Dessert / evening | Gift exchange, or a soft gratitude go-around |

You don't need every block. One structured game plus one free-form stretch is enough for most houses.

## Group-size cheat sheet

| Guests | What works |
|---|---|
| 6–8 | Pairs or two teams; duties wheel; white elephant |
| 9–14 | Three teams for trivia; bingo; Secret Santa |
| 15–24 | Four teams or stations; bingo with player links; two gift-exchange tables if needed |

Above about 24, split into rooms or rounds so half the house isn't waiting for a turn.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I stop couples always ending up on the same team?

Say beforehand that you'll allow one reshuffle if a couple lands together when you wanted mixed tables. Or put each couple in a "tier" the same size as the number of teams so they can't share a team—see the [balanced teams guide](/guides/how-to-make-balanced-random-teams).

### What if someone arrives late?

Add their name and reshuffle only that game, or seat them on the smallest team by hand and say so. Don't rerun a gift exchange after links are already sent.

### Can kids and adults play on the same teams?

Yes for bingo, stations and photo scavenger. For trivia, either mix ages on purpose or run a kids' round with easier prompts while adults clear the table.

### Do I need an account for these tools?

No. ExesTools runs in the browser with no signup. Lists stay on that device until you clear them.

### What if people hate competitive games?

Stick to bingo, duties spins and a soft go-around. Skip scored trivia. The point is mixing the room, not crowning a winner.
