# White Elephant rules: the standard game, steal limits, dice and big groups

By Arhum Waheed · Published 2026-11-03 · Updated 2026-11-03

White Elephant (also called Yankee Swap or Dirty Santa) is the gift exchange where nobody knows what they will go home with. That is the fun of it, and also the source of every argument. Most White Elephant games that go wrong don't go wrong because of the gifts. They go wrong because three people learned three different versions of the rules, and nobody said which one counts before the first gift came off the pile.

This guide gives you a version you can read out loud in under a minute, the house rules worth deciding in advance, a dice variant for groups who hate stealing, and changes that keep a 30-person office game under an hour.

## The standard rules, in the order you'd say them

Read these to the group before anyone touches a gift:

1. **Everyone brings one wrapped gift** within the agreed budget, with no name on it. All gifts go in one pile.
2. **Everyone gets a number.** That number is your turn.
3. **Player 1 opens a gift** from the pile, in front of everyone.
4. **Every player after that has a choice:** open a new wrapped gift, or steal a gift someone has already opened.
5. **If your gift is stolen,** you immediately open a new gift or steal a different one. You can't steal back the gift that was just taken from you.
6. **A gift is frozen after its third steal.** Whoever holds it then keeps it.
7. **When the last wrapped gift is opened,** player 1 gets one final chance to swap with anyone (frozen gifts excepted). Then the game is over.

That's it. Everything else is a house rule, and the house rules are where you avoid trouble.

## Drawing the turn order fairly

Turn order matters more than people expect. Going first is a disadvantage, since you open blind and have nothing to steal, which is why player 1 gets the final swap. Going last is the best seat in the house, because you can choose from every opened gift. People notice who gets that seat, so the draw should be one nobody can question.

Three ways to do it:

- **Paper slips in a bowl.** Fine for 8 people. Slow for 25, and someone always palms a slip or drops one.
- **Random name picker, one name at a time.** Put every player's name into the [random name picker](/random-name-picker) and turn on remove-winner. Each spin takes the next turn. Because each name leaves the wheel after it's picked, nobody can be drawn twice, and the room watches every pick happen. Write the order on a whiteboard as you go.
- **Number draw.** If people are already seated, set the [random number wheel](/random-number-wheel) to 1 through the number of players and draw without repeats. Go around the circle, and each person spins for their own number.

If guests are arriving at different times, draw the order before the party and send it round in the group chat. A share link to your name list helps if someone else is running the spin on the day.

## House rules to decide before you start

Settle these out loud. Each one is something I've seen start a real argument.

### How many steals before a gift freezes?

Three is standard. Two is better for groups over 20, because every steal adds a turn. Unlimited steals sounds fun until one good gift circles the room for fifteen minutes.

### Can a gift be stolen twice in one turn?

Most groups say no: once a gift has been stolen during a turn, it is safe until the next numbered player goes. Without this rule, two people can trade the same gift back and forth forever.

### Does player 1 get the final swap?

Keep it in. Without it, going first is just a penalty. If your group thinks it's too powerful, let player 1 choose to go last instead.

### Can people open their own gift?

Usually yes, it just goes the same as any other gift. Some groups forbid it, but you can't enforce it unless gifts are marked, and marking gifts defeats the point.

### What about the gag gifts?

Decide whether joke gifts are welcome. In a family game with kids, a rule like "something you'd actually use" saves disappointment. At a work party, joke gifts are part of the culture at some companies and a problem at others. If in doubt, say "no gag gifts" in the invite.

## The dice version (no steal counting)

If your group dislikes stealing, or you just want something faster and sillier, play with dice.

1. Everyone opens a gift first, so everyone is holding one.
2. Go round the circle. Each player rolls one die and does what it says.
3. Play two rounds (three for fewer than 8 people), then stop.

A common rule sheet:

| Roll | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Swap with the person on your left |
| 2 | Swap with the person on your right |
| 3 | Swap with anyone |
| 4 | Everyone passes their gift one seat left |
| 5 | Everyone passes their gift one seat right |
| 6 | Keep what you have |

Two things make it work. First, **decide the number of rounds before the first roll**, because the dice game has no natural ending. Second, put the rule sheet where everyone can see it, so nobody has to ask what a 4 means.

No die in the house? Set the [random number wheel](/random-number-wheel) to 1–6 with repeats allowed and spin it on a phone in the middle of the table. Swap in your own actions if you like: "everyone passes two seats" or "swap with whoever's wearing the most red" both go down well.

## Rules for big groups (25 people and up)

Every steal is an extra turn, so time grows faster than the guest list. A 12-person game takes about 30 minutes. A 30-person game with three-steal limits can easily run past 75. For big groups:

- **Freeze after two steals**, not three.
- **One steal per turn.** After a steal, the person who lost their gift has to open a new one.
- **No steals in the last five turns**, or limit those turns to opening only. The last few turns are where the long chains happen.
- **Put opened gifts on a numbered table** so people can see what's available without asking.
- **Over about 40 people, split into two circles**, each with its own pile and host. Two 20-person games running side by side finish sooner than one 40-person game.
- **Draw the order before people arrive.** Passing a hat around 30 people takes longer than you think.

## Remote and hybrid White Elephant

Video-call White Elephant works if gifts are bought online and shipped after the game:

1. Everyone picks a gift within budget and posts a photo of the wrapped box, or a mystery description, in a shared doc.
2. Draw the turn order on screen with the [random name picker](/random-name-picker), with remove-winner on.
3. Players "open" a gift by having its buyer reveal it on camera.
4. Steals work the same way. Keep a running list of who holds what in the doc.
5. After the game, each buyer ships their gift to whoever ended up with it.

Agree on shipping before the game. "The buyer pays to ship it" is the simplest rule.

## White Elephant vs Secret Santa

They get mixed up, but they're different games. In **Secret Santa**, each person is privately assigned one specific person to buy for, so gifts can be personal. In **White Elephant**, nobody is assigned anyone; gifts are generic and the game decides who gets what.

Pick Secret Santa when people know each other well and want thoughtful gifts. Pick White Elephant when the group is mixed, or when the game is the point. ## Budget and gift guidance that saves the night

- **State a range, not a maximum.** "$20–30" gets more even gifts than "under $30," where someone always brings a $4 candle.
- **Suggest gifts that work for anyone.** Good snacks, a nice blanket, a board game, a coffee gift card or a decent water bottle. These are the gifts that get stolen.
- **Say whether used or regifted items are OK.** Some groups love a regift round. Others feel short-changed. Either is fine as long as it's in the invite.

## A game for the people waiting their turn

In a big game, players can sit for 20 minutes between turns. A round of holiday bingo, played on the side, keeps them involved. ## Frequently asked questions

### How many people do you need for White Elephant?

Six or more for the standard game, because with fewer players there's not much to steal. The dice version works from four.

### How many times can a gift be stolen in White Elephant?

Usually three, then the gift is frozen with whoever has it. For groups over 20, use two. Whatever you choose, announce it before the first gift is opened.

### Does the first person get to steal at the end?

In most versions, yes. Player 1 gets one final swap after the last gift is opened, because they started with no choice. Frozen gifts can't be taken.

### How do you pick the order for White Elephant?

Any fair random draw will do: slips from a bowl, or a name picker with remove-winner on, so each name is drawn only once and everyone sees each pick. For large groups, draw the order before the party.

### How long does White Elephant take?

Roughly 2 to 3 minutes per player with a three-steal limit. A group of 15 takes 30 to 45 minutes. For bigger groups, use two-steal limits and one steal per turn, or split into two circles.
