# How to run a fair online giveaway

By Arhum Waheed · Published 2026-10-13 · Updated 2026-10-13

Most giveaway complaints are not about the draw itself. They come from things decided too late: a rule nobody wrote down, an entry window that ended in a different time zone than people assumed, or a winner who went quiet and a host who quietly picked someone else. A random picker solves only one step. The rest is planning, and that is what this guide covers.

I build the picking tools on ExesTools, so I will be specific about what they do and what they don't. They pick fairly from the list you give them. They don't collect entries, check eligibility or make a giveaway legal where you live. ExesTools is a free tool, not a certified drawing service. If your platform or local law requires a certified or supervised draw, use one.

## Step 1: Write the rules before you announce anything

Write the rules first and post them with the giveaway, pinned or linked, not buried in a reply. Once entries start, any rule you add looks like it was written to favour someone.

A rules post that heads off most disputes covers these points:

- **Who can enter.** Age, country or region, and whether staff, moderators or family members are excluded.
- **How to enter.** "Comment with your favourite flavour" is clear. "Like, share and tag three friends" is hard to check by hand, and some platforms restrict tagging and sharing requirements, so check their promotion rules first.
- **How many entries each person gets.** One per person is easiest to defend. If extra actions earn extra entries, say exactly how many each one is worth.
- **The entry window, with a time zone.** "Closes Friday 16 October, 11:59 pm PKT (UTC+5)" leaves no room for argument. "Closes Friday" leaves plenty.
- **The prizes, in draw order.** Say which prize is drawn first and whether each person can win only once.
- **How the winner is picked.** For example: "Winners are drawn at random on a livestream on Saturday at 8 pm, using an online random picker."
- **How winners are contacted and how long they have to reply.** 48 or 72 hours is common. Say whether you will draw a replacement if they don't respond.
- **What you will publish.** A first name or handle is usually enough. Never promise to publish personal details.

## Step 2: Collect and clean the entry list

This is where fairness is most often lost, usually by accident.

ExesTools doesn't connect to Instagram, YouTube or any other account, and it doesn't import comments. You copy the entrants yourself, from a comment export, a form's response sheet or your own notes, and paste them one per line. That manual step is an advantage: it forces you to look at the list.

Clean it in this order:

1. **Remove anyone who doesn't qualify** under the rules you published, such as entries after the deadline or from outside the eligible region. Keep a short note of who you removed and why.
2. **Apply your entry limit.** If the rule is one entry per person, remove duplicates. On the wheel tools this happens automatically: duplicate lines are skipped, and capital letters don't count as a difference, so "Sam" and "sam" become one slice. It also means you can't give someone extra chances by pasting their name twice on a wheel.
3. **Split people who share a name.** Two different entrants called Ayesha need handles or initials, or the wheel will treat them as one person.
4. **Count the result.** Write the final number of entrants in your notes. If you announce "214 entries" and later someone counts 230 comments, you can explain the 16 you removed.

Each ExesTools picker is set up for a different kind of giveaway. All of them pick at random with the browser's secure random number generator, but they handle lists differently.

### A big list, several winners, or numbered tickets: raffle generator

If you have more names than a wheel's 60-slice limit, need several winners and alternates in one draw, or are drawing from numbered tickets, use the [raffle generator](/raffle-generator). ### Spinning for the prize, not the person

Some hosts draw the winner first, then spin a second wheel of prizes to see what they get. That works well on streams. Load **Prize rewards** or **Stream giveaway** on the prize wheel and edit the list. Remember every slice has the same chance, so a gift card and a sticker are equally likely. Plan your budget around that.

## Step 4: Do the draw where people can see it

A draw nobody saw is a draw you will be asked to prove. Pick one of these and say in your rules which one you'll use:

- **Live on stream.** Put the wheel on screen before you start. Read the number of entrants out loud, scroll through the list, then spin.
- **Screen recording.** Record your whole screen from the moment you paste the list to the moment the winner appears. Don't cut the video. Post it, or keep it in case someone asks.
- **Share the exact list.** On the wheel tools, **Copy share link** creates a link to the exact wheel you spun. Anyone with the link can see every name on it, so only share it if your entrants are comfortable with their handles being visible.

The raffle generator adds a written record: the date and time on your device, a draw code, the number of names and tickets, the rules used, each winner with their prize and ticket number, and the alternates. It also shows a **list fingerprint**, a short code made from your final entry list. If you publish the list before the draw, anyone can paste it into the page and check that the fingerprint matches. That proves the list didn't change. It doesn't prove anything about how the winner was picked, so pair it with a recording.

One thing worth knowing about the wheels: the result is chosen first, then the animation spins to land on it. How long or how hard the wheel spins changes nothing, so don't let anyone tell you a "better spin" exists.

## Step 5: Announce winners and handle no-shows

Announce in the same place you ran the giveaway. Post the handle, the prize and when they need to reply by. Then contact the winner directly, and be clear that you will never ask for a password or a payment to release a prize. Scammers copy giveaway accounts and message entrants with fake "you won" notes, so warn your audience in the announcement too.

Decide before the draw what happens if a winner doesn't reply. There are two fair options:

1. **Draw alternates at the same time as the winners.** The raffle generator does this in order: if the winner doesn't claim, Alternate 1 is offered the prize, then Alternate 2. If you need another backup later, **Draw another alternate** adds one without changing anyone already drawn.
2. **Redraw on camera** from the same list, minus the previous winner. On a wheel, leave **Remove winner after spin** on, and the non-responder is already off the wheel.

The unfair option is picking someone yourself because they seem keen. Even if it is well meant, it is the fastest way to lose trust.

## A worked example

Say you run a giveaway with 3 prizes and 140 commenters. After cleaning you have 128 eligible entrants (one entry each).

- In the raffle generator, paste the 128 names (One entry per name on), set 3 winners and 2 alternates, and draw.
- Each entrant has 1/128 ≈ 0.78% chance of winning the first pick. Say that in your rules if you publish odds.
- With **Each person can win only once** on (the default), a winner leaves the draw, so nobody can take two of the three prizes.
- Copy or print the record and keep it with your recording.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use ExesTools for an Instagram or YouTube giveaway?

Yes, as long as you follow that platform's promotion rules. ExesTools doesn't connect to your account or import comments, so copy the entrant names yourself, paste them in, and spin or draw on screen.

### Can I paste the same name twice for a bonus entry?

Not on the wheels: duplicate lines are skipped. On the raffle generator, keep One entry per name on for social giveaways so each person appears once. If your rules truly allow extra entries, say so in the published rules and use a method you can show on camera (for example separate ticket numbers). Do not rely on pasting a name twice on a wheel.

### What should I do if the winner never replies?

Follow the claim window in your published rules. If you drew alternates, offer the prize to Alternate 1. If not, redraw on camera from the same list without the previous winner.

### Is an ExesTools draw legally valid?

ExesTools is a free random picker, not a certified drawing service, and nothing here is legal advice. Giveaway and raffle laws vary by country and region. If a certified draw is required, use one.

### Can entrants check the list I drew from?

Yes. On a wheel, Copy share link gives a link to the exact list you spun. In the raffle generator, publish your list before the draw and anyone can compare the list fingerprint on the record.
