# White elephant gift exchange rules (and the variations that actually work)

By Arhum Waheed · Published 2026-10-26 · Updated 2026-10-26

White elephant is the gift exchange where you can lose the thing you wanted to your aunt. That's the point. It's also why it goes sideways: half the room thinks steals are unlimited, the other half thinks you can only steal once, and somebody's uncle insists his family has always let the last person swap with anyone.

Pick your rules before the first gift is opened, read them out loud, and the game runs itself. Here's a version you can read out, plus the decisions you'll need to make and how to pick a turn order nobody can argue with.

## The rules in under a minute

You can read this out word for word:

1. Everyone brought one wrapped gift, under the agreed budget. All gifts go in a pile in the middle.
2. We'll go in a random order. When it's your turn, you either **open a new gift from the pile** or **steal an opened gift** from someone else.
3. If your gift gets stolen, you do the same thing right away: open a new gift or steal a different one. You can't steal back the gift that was just taken from you.
4. A gift can be stolen **three times**. After the third steal, it's frozen and stays with whoever has it.
5. When the last gift has been opened, the person who went first gets one final chance to swap with anyone.
6. Then we're done. No trades after the game unless both people want to.

That's the most common version. If your group has played before with different rules, ask the room once and go with the majority. Don't negotiate rule by rule.

## The decisions you need to make first

### How many steals before a gift is frozen?

Three is the usual number. Two makes the game noticeably faster, which matters with more than about 20 people. Unlimited steals sounds fun until the same bottle of fancy olive oil changes hands eleven times and the game takes ninety minutes. I'd never run unlimited steals with more than ten players.

Whatever you pick, make the count visible. A sticky note on the gift with a tally mark per steal saves a lot of "wait, was that two or three?"

### Can you steal on your very first turn?

The usual answer is no. Player one has nothing to steal, so they open. After that, anyone can steal any opened, unfrozen gift. Some groups add "no stealing until three gifts are open." It's a good rule for kids' parties, where the first opened toy is a magnet.

### Does the first player get a final swap?

Yes, usually, and here's why: going first is the worst position in the game. You open blind, and everyone gets a chance to take your gift before you ever get to choose. The final swap balances it out. Without it, people quietly hope they're drawn last.

### What's the budget?

Say a number and say whether it's a ceiling or a target. "$20, try to get close" leads to better gifts than "under $20," which somehow produces a lot of $4 novelty mugs. If your group loves joke gifts, say so. If it doesn't, say that too. Mixing a $20 gag gift with a $20 nice gift is where hurt feelings come from.

## Setting a fair turn order

Turn order decides a lot in this game, so pick it at random where everyone can see.

The paper way is numbers in a hat. It works, but someone always unfolds two slips stuck together, and the person drawing number 1 groans loudly.

A quicker way: open the [random name picker](/random-name-picker), paste everyone's name one per line, and leave **remove winner** on (it's the default there). Spin until one name is left. The order names come off the wheel is the order people play, and the last name on the wheel goes last. Put the laptop where the room can see it, or cast it to the TV. If two guests share a first name, add an initial, because duplicate lines on the wheel are skipped.

Write the order on a sheet of paper as you spin. The wheel doesn't keep a history, and you'll want the list in front of you for the whole game.

## A worked round with six players

Six friends, $20 budget, three-steal limit, first player gets a final swap. The wheel gives this order: Jo, Marcus, Priya, Dan, Lee, Sam.

- **Jo** opens a gift: a heated blanket.
- **Marcus** steals the blanket (steal 1). Jo, now empty-handed, opens a new gift: a board game.
- **Priya** opens a gift: a coffee sampler.
- **Dan** steals the blanket from Marcus (steal 2). Marcus can't steal it straight back, so he steals the coffee from Priya. Priya opens a new gift: a candle.
- **Lee** steals the blanket from Dan (steal 3). It's frozen now. Dan opens a new gift: a plant.
- **Sam** opens the last gift: a hot sauce set.

All gifts are open. Jo gets the final swap and trades the board game for Sam's hot sauce. Done. Six players, six turns, about 15 minutes, and the blanket finishes with Lee.

Notice that Jo, who went first and lost the blanket immediately, still got to choose last. That's the final swap doing its job.

## Variations worth knowing

| Variation | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Two-steal limit** | Frozen after two steals instead of three | Groups of 20+ |
| **Dice white elephant** | Everyone opens a gift, then players roll and pass, swap or steal by the number | Big groups who don't want 40 separate turns |
| **Steal only on even turns** | Odd-numbered turns must open | Kids' parties, shy crowds |
| **Theme exchange** | Every gift fits a theme: "something cozy," "something under $15 from a local shop" | Offices, mixed-age families |
| **Yankee swap** | Another name for the same game in parts of the US | Everyone, once you've explained it's the same thing |

For the dice version, use a real die or a number wheel set to 1–6. Write the meaning of each number on a card first: 1–2 pass your gift left, 3–4 pass right, 5 swap with anyone, 6 do nothing.

## White elephant vs Secret Santa

They get mixed up constantly. In white elephant, nobody is assigned a person; you bring a gift for the pile and anyone could end up with it. In Secret Santa, you draw one specific person and buy for them. If your group wants named gifts with couple exclusions, run the draw on the [Secret Santa generator](/secret-santa-generator) and send each person their own match link instead of passing a hat.

Some offices do both: Secret Santa for a small team, white elephant for the whole floor party.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people do you need for white elephant?

It works with as few as four or five, but it's more fun with eight to twenty. Past about 25, switch to a two-steal limit or the dice version, or it runs too long.

### Can you steal a gift back right after it's taken from you?

In the standard rules, no. You have to open a new gift or steal a different one. That one rule stops two people trading the same gift back and forth forever.

### How do I choose who goes first in white elephant?

Pick the whole order at random in front of the group. A name picker with remove winner on gives you a complete order in one go: the order names leave the wheel is the order people play.

### What happens to the last person in white elephant?

They have the most choice, because every opened gift that isn't frozen is available to steal. That's why the first player usually gets a final swap at the end.

### What's a good white elephant budget?

Whatever most guests can spend without thinking about it. Say clearly whether it's a target or a maximum, and whether joke gifts are welcome.
